# M3 User-Testing Plan

Testing will determine whether students understand CourseGuide AI, find its
guidance useful, and receive the appropriate amount of help without having
the assignment completed for them.

The team will recruit five external testers who fit the target profile:
undergraduate students in reasoning-intensive or quantitative STEM courses
who have used tutoring tools, search engines, or general-purpose AI. Team
members and the instructor will not be used as testers. Sessions will last
approximately 20 minutes and use an in-person or video format. Use anonymous
labels and do not record full names or unnecessary personal information.

The tester will be asked:

> Submit a course-related question that you would normally ask a tutor or
> chatbot, review the response, and explain whether the next step is useful.

The facilitator will observe the tester's actions, hesitation, confusion,
understanding of the next step, opinion of the help level, and willingness to
use the system again. The facilitator will ask:

1. What were you expecting the agent to do before you started?
2. Was the response clear?
3. What did you think the agent wanted you to do next?
4. Where did you hesitate or feel uncertain?
5. Was the response at the right level of help?
6. Did the agent provide too much or too little information?
7. What would make you trust this agent?
8. Would you use the agent again for a similar course question?

For each session, record the tester role and context, date and format, task,
actions, points of confusion, surprising behavior, whether the tester would
use the agent again, and suggested improvements.

Proposed success criteria are:

- At least four of five testers complete the task without facilitator help.
- At least four testers can explain the next step they received.
- At least three testers would use the agent again for a similar question.
- No critical policy violations occur.
- At least four testers understand that the system guides reasoning rather
  than completing assignments.

Track repeated confusion, requests for complete answers, privacy concerns,
dissatisfaction with limited assistance, and whether users understand the
Planner/Tutor roles. These findings will support the M4 decision to proceed,
pivot, or narrow the target user and use case.
